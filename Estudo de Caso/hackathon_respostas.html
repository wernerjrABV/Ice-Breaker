Template 1 - Project overview

1. Project in one sentence

We are building a Generative AI agent for cooler maintenance, serving first-level support and points of sale to resolve eligible cases remotely, reduce unnecessary technical dispatches, and improve asset traceability.

2. Problem or opportunity

Cooler maintenance requests can trigger unnecessary field visits because initial triage is not fully standardized. This affects support teams, suppliers, technicians, and points of sale. In the last three months, 67,448 tickets were analyzed: 14,324 showed remote-resolution potential, while 30,141 indicated a real technical intervention.

3. Proposed solution

We propose a GenAI first-level support agent that uses ticket history, technical manuals, and guided diagnostics to classify requests, guide simple safe checks, and recommend remote resolution or technician dispatch. Photos of labels or serial numbers may improve equipment identification and traceability.

4. GenAI & technology approach

The agent will use GenAI/RAG over maintenance knowledge and diagnostic rules, with optional computer vision for labels or serial numbers. It will provide guided recommendations and escalate cases when needed. Specific AI model, platform, orchestration, and architecture: Not provided.

GenAI agent for cooler-maintenance triage that uses tickets, manuals, and guided diagnostics to resolve eligible cases remotely, reduce avoidable technical visits, improve request quality, and increase asset traceability.

Business challenge

Reduce unnecessary technician dispatches for cooler maintenance and improve the quality of triage, diagnosis, and equipment traceability in the field.

Current baseline

  • Current workflow: A maintenance ticket is opened and a supplier/technician is activated; initial triage does not appear to be fully standardized, automated, or supported by an intelligent knowledge base.
  • Pain points: Incomplete information or incorrect equipment identification reduces diagnostic accuracy; simple occurrences may follow the same service flow as complex cases; traceability depends on master data and manual records.
  • Available evidence: 67,448 tickets analyzed over three months; 14,324 with potential for remote resolution; 30,141 with a strong indication of real technical intervention.
  • Existing tools: Not provided.

Answers to review questions

1. How did we reach the BRL 7M OPEX figure? Where does the reduction come from? What are the TAM and the cut?

The approximately BRL 7M figure is the 60% capture scenario: 14,324 tickets with remote-resolution potential, annualized from the three-month analysis, multiplied by the average service cost of BRL 200, and then multiplied by 60%: (14,324 / 3) x 12 x BRL 200 x 60% = BRL 6,875,520. The reduction comes from avoided technical visits, not from a confirmed FTE reduction. The full annual TAM is BRL 11,459,200 (100% of the eligible-ticket potential); the scope is 14,324 of 67,448 tickets, and the proposed capture cut is 50% or 60%.

2. How will the pilot be measured?

The pilot should compare the eligible cases handled by the agent with the current process, tracking remote-resolution rate, avoided technician dispatches, escalation accuracy, resolution time, average cost per case, and user adoption. Pilot duration, sample size, targets, owner, and final measurement baseline are not provided.

3. What is the real benefit: FTE reduction, cooler reduction, or reuse?

The benefit currently supported by the available evidence is OPEX cost avoidance from fewer unnecessary technical visits. There is no confirmed evidence yet of FTE reduction, cooler reduction, or cooler reuse. Any benefit from productivity, asset reuse, or cooler reduction must be validated during the pilot.

4. Why not challenge the current BDR solution (BEES FORCE / AS-MOBI)?

The intended approach is to use the Delfos infrastructure for active and reactive sending. The available information does not document the functional overlap, gaps, or comparative business case versus BDR, BEES FORCE, or AS-MOBI. A formal comparison and confirmation of the integration boundary are still required.
